# Decision Mapping Canvas

**Connect decisions to measures, responsibilities, and actions.**

The **Decision Mapping Canvas** is a structured workshop tool for clarifying which business decision needs support **before** selecting KPIs, designing dashboards, or building semantic models.

Its guiding principle is:

> **Decision before metric. Action before reporting.**

## Purpose

A metric is useful only when its role in a decision is understood. This canvas helps teams identify the decision to be made, who owns it, when it occurs, which options are available, what information may trigger action, and which risks require attention.

Use it to make the link between **business decisions, information needs, and actions** explicit.

## The eight sections

| Section | Key question |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Decision** | What exactly needs to be decided, and what cannot be decided without the information? |
| **2. Decision maker** | Who is accountable for the decision, and who must be able to act? |
| **3. Decision timing** | When is the decision made, and when must the information be available? |
| **4. Action options** | Which possible responses exist, and by what criteria are they compared? |
| **5. Trigger metric** | Which measure is decisive, and how is it defined? |
| **6. Signal or threshold** | What deviation, trend, or boundary calls for attention or escalation? |
| **7. Action** | What concrete action should follow, and who takes it? |
| **8. Context and risks** | Which assumptions, interpretation risks, or limitations could lead to an incorrect decision? |

## When to use it

- At the beginning of a BI or analytics initiative, before requirements are translated into a report design.
- When stakeholders request a new KPI but its decision relevance is unclear.
- When a dashboard produces information without a clear next action.
- When measures, thresholds, decision ownership, or escalation rules need to be revisited.

## Suggested workflow

1. Discuss a **specific decision**, not a general reporting wish.
2. Identify the decision maker, timing, and available courses of action.
3. Determine which metric genuinely influences that decision. Distinguish the decisive measure from supporting context measures.
4. Agree on meaningful signals and thresholds **with the relevant business owners**. Avoid assuming that a threshold is valid simply because it is technically easy to calculate.
5. Record the intended actions, relevant limitations, and open questions.
6. Transfer the resulting information needs to the [Dashboard Canvas](../../02-requirements/dashboard-canvas/README.md), if a dashboard or report is the intended solution.

## Expected output

A shared description of the **decision context**: decision, responsible people, timing, action options, trigger measures, signals, possible actions, and interpretation risks.

The canvas helps **clarify requirements**. It does not itself approve KPI definitions, certify data quality, or constitute a formal contract.

## Relationship to the other resources

| Resource | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Decision Mapping Canvas** | Clarifies **why** information is needed and **what decision or action** it should support. |
| [**Dashboard Canvas**](../../02-requirements/dashboard-canvas/README.md) | Describes **what a specific dashboard must answer and provide** for its users. |
| **Semantic Modeling Guideline** | Guides **how business definitions and metrics are modeled, documented, tested, and governed** in Power BI. |

The Decision Mapping Canvas and Dashboard Canvas are complementary, **not mandatory sequential gates**. A semantic model may serve several decisions and several reports.
